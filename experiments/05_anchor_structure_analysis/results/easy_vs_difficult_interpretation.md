# High-clipping easy vs difficult anchors — v0.3

## Question

Why do many anchor futures with many negative entries (hence many clipping-capable coordinates) remain `chi=1`, while others become `chi>1`?

## Result

High clipping is not enough. Three ingredients must coincide:

1. **Clipping capacity:** the anchor update contains coordinates at which distinct current weights can collapse to the same anchor future.
2. **Reachable variation:** the anchor fiber actually contains two or more reachable current fields that differ on such hidden coordinates.
3. **Revealing co-observation:** at least one alternative action is observed for both fields and makes an agent whose row differs between those fields a winner.

The third condition is the decisive separator between high-clipping easy and difficult groups.

Using the winner-row criterion established in v0.2, all 625 anchor groups satisfy the exact equivalence

`chi(G_i) > 1  iff  there exists a within-anchor field pair and a co-observed action that reveals a differing winner row.`

This is an exact characterization of whether an anchor fiber has any internal incompatibility edge; it is not a statistical classifier.

## High-clipping comparison

For the descriptive threshold `anchor_neg >= 16`, there are 140 anchor groups: 99 easy (`chi=1`) and 41 difficult (`chi>1`).

The easy groups are not simply singletons or invariant fibers. They have on average 2.81 reachable fields, 5.76 variable coordinates, and 1.68 variable rows. Some are highly variable: future 2140 has 16 reachable fields and all 16 clipping-capable coordinates vary, yet remains `chi=1`.

The difference is action-conditioned observability. Across all 99 high-clipping easy groups, the number of revealing co-observed actions is exactly zero. Across the 41 difficult groups it averages 5.22, with an average of 8.49 revealable field pairs.

Thus the missing ingredient in high-clipping easy groups is not hidden variation itself. It is **a co-observed action capable of exposing that variation**.

## Example: future 2140

This is a particularly useful counterexample to the idea that clipping or reachable variation alone causes difficulty.

- anchor negative entries: 16
- reachable fields in anchor fiber: 16
- variable coordinates: 16
- variable rows: 4
- actions observed somewhere in the group: all 32
- revealing co-observed actions: 0
- chromatic number: 1

Even though the group as a whole has broad action coverage, the relevant action coverage is **pair-specific**. Whenever two fields differ in a row, the actions co-observed for that particular pair never make that row a winning row. Their hidden distinction therefore never affects the specified next-field prediction on the actually observed domain.

## Interpretation

The 625+67 correction is generated by an interaction of three structures rather than by a static property of the anchor future alone:

`clipping non-injectivity × reachable field variants × action-conditioned revealability`.

This also explains why static descriptors such as negative-entry count or symmetry cannot by themselves determine difficulty. They describe the capacity to hide distinctions, but not whether the reachable transition domain ever asks the prediction task to distinguish them.

The result is strongly aligned with the paper's central theme: predictive distinctions are task- and domain-dependent. Two present fields may be microscopically different and may even differ on many clipped coordinates, yet remain predictively equivalent if no co-observed action exposes those differences.

## What this does and does not establish

This establishes an exact criterion for `chi>1` (presence of internal conflict) in the anchor fibers of this finite ABCW instance. It does **not** by itself determine the full value of `chi(G_i)` once conflicts exist. Explaining why difficult fibers require 2, 3, 4, or 7 colors remains the next structural question.
